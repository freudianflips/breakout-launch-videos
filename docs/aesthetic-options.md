# Aesthetic options: Plays, round 4

The owner's direction: "more modern, more unexpected, more 'cool' and modern and conceptual. Fast cuts, shorter videos, one crisp message."

Every option below is **15 s at 128 BPM (8 bars)**, with a cut every beat or half beat at its peak (about 20 to 30 cuts). It carries **one message**, uses no narrator, and shows real Plays UI as fragments, never a whole-screen tour. All of them can be made free (code-drawn, synthesised sound). Paid generation is optional, and only where noted.

## The one message (pick 1)

| | Line | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **M1** | **Signal in. Meeting out.** | Four words. The whole product: any signal goes in, a booked meeting comes out. |
| M2 | Three visitors. One account. One email. | Rides the "Multiple Visitors, One Account" play straight from the UI. |

Every option ends on the wordmark, **Plays**, and the chosen line.

## A. Type slam

**The idea:** the film is made almost entirely of type. One giant word fills the frame, then cuts hard to the next on every beat: SIGNAL. VISIT. PRICING. 3 PEOPLE. ONE ACCOUNT. → EMAIL → MEETING. The rhythm says the message before you read it.

- **Look:** pure white and near-black, with one electric violet `#4c00ff` hit per bar. Words crop off the frame edges, set at 600 to 900 px.
- **Type:** Manrope 800, very tight tracking. One italic serif word (New Kansas) as the twist on the last beat.
- **Motion:** hard cuts only, no easing between shots. Each word snaps with a 2-frame scale overshoot and a 1-frame inverted flash on the downbeats.
- **Product:** 3 macro crops of real UI, each 0.5 s, cut into the type: the "Visitor Count ≥ 3" field, a `{team_research_line}` token, the Test button.
- **Sound:** a dry, clipped electronic beat. A typewriter-key hit on every word; the bass drops out for the last word.
- **Reference feel:** Nike and Apple type films, Swiss poster design.

## B. Signal field

**The idea:** 700+ signals as a living field of points (visits, hires, funding, job moves), each a dot with a tiny label. They drift, then snap together into 3 dots (3 visitors), then 1 account, then 1 envelope, then 1 calendar block. The film is the data organising itself.

- **Look:** a black ground with violet and white points, hairline mono labels (PRICING · 09:41, HIRING · VP SALES). The feel is a scientific instrument, very clean.
- **Type:** small mono labels everywhere and one big line at the end in Manrope. Minimal.
- **Motion:** fast particle snaps on the beat, with smooth flocking between. The camera dives through the field.
- **Product:** the real email canvas appears where the particles land; its tokens are drawn as particle clusters.
- **Sound:** granular clicks per signal and a sub pulse on each snap. Glassy and modern.
- **Reference feel:** Teenage Engineering, a data-art installation, an Apple chip reveal.

## C. POV, screen-native

**The idea:** native to LinkedIn and X. A fast, captioned screen recording that feels made by a person, not an agency. "POV: 3 people from the same account just looked at your pricing page." Then the play builds itself in 6 quick zooms and a calendar invite lands. It's funny, quick and real.

- **Look:** the real Breakout UI full screen with punchy zooms. Bold white captions with a black outline, a "✓" sticker or two. Vertical 9:16 first, with a 16:9 cut.
- **Type:** Manrope 800 captions, 2 to 4 words at a time, timed to the beat.
- **Motion:** jump zooms (110% to 180%) and quick whip-pans between UI regions.
- **Product:** the hero. Every cut is real UI: templates, the chat prompt, Visitor Count ≥ 3, tokens, Test. **This needs one real recording or a demo workspace with sample data**, so the screens can be shown unblurred.
- **Sound:** a trending-style lo-fi beat, UI clicks and a "ding" on the meeting.
- **Reference feel:** founder-led SaaS launch posts, Linear and Raycast social clips.

## D. Departures board

**The idea:** signals arrive like flights. A split-flap board clatters through the incoming signals (PRICING PAGE · 3 VISITORS · ACME), then the board flips to the departures side: EMAIL 1 · SENT, MEETING · BOOKED 10:30. "Signal in. Meeting out." is literally the board.

- **Look:** a black board with cream flaps and one violet status light. Sharp, graphic and mechanical: modern-retro without the grain.
- **Type:** split-flap characters (a mono grotesk), then the wordmark on the last flip.
- **Motion:** cascading flaps on every 16th note, and hard cuts between macro shots of single flaps and the full board.
- **Product:** 2 inserts of real UI (the filter "3" and the Test button), framed as the "gate" screens.
- **Sound:** real-feeling flap clatter (synthesised), a chime per row, a deep "boarding" tone on the meeting.
- **Reference feel:** a Vignelli-style airport, Dieter Rams, mechanical precision.

## E. Cutout collage

**The idea:** a fast editorial collage. Real UI pieces are cut out like paper and slammed onto bold colour fields with stickers, arrows and scribbles: one signal becomes a stack of evidence, which becomes an email, which becomes a meeting. Loud, confident, very current.

- **Look:** flat colour blocks (electric violet, hot pink `#ff4fd8`, cream, black), hard drop shadows, cut-paper edges, hand-drawn arrows.
- **Type:** huge Manrope 800 stacked words, one New Kansas italic word per scene.
- **Motion:** stop-motion feel (12 fps on the collage pieces) against smooth 30 fps type. Pieces slap in on the beat, with a lot of motion per second.
- **Product:** cutouts of the real UI: the template card, the chat bubble, the "3", the email canvas, the Test button.
- **Sound:** paper slaps, a marker squeak, a bouncy drum machine.
- **Reference feel:** Spotify Wrapped, Figma Config titles, a modern magazine.

## How we pick

1. Choose 1 message (M1, M2 or M3).
2. Choose 1 or 2 aesthetics.
3. I make a **5-second look test** of each pick (not a full film) so you can judge motion and pace. Then a 15 s storyboard for the winner, then the redo.

My recommendation: **M1 with A (Type slam) or D (Departures board).** Both are the most unexpected for B2B, deliver one message in 15 s, and need nothing we don't have. C is the most native to LinkedIn, but it needs a demo workspace recording.
